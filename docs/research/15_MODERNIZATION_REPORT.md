# Harden RVC's Spine, Then Distil Its Successor

DEXAIV's real-time engine is already one of the better-engineered RVC v2 deployments in existence: per-stage CUDA graphs cut the 20 ms hop from ~38 ms eager to **15.3 ms p50 / 20.7 ms p99** on the RTX 5060 Ti, the loopback round trip measures **72–110 ms**, and the synthesizer is bit-exact against rvc-webui ([docs/DEVIATIONS.md](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/docs/DEVIATIONS.md)). The remaining artificiality is therefore not a compute problem and mostly not a streaming problem; it comes from the RVC recipe itself (ContentVec leaks ~28% recoverable speaker identity, the NSF-HiFi-GAN decoder is driven by a hard binary voicing gate and a hand-crafted sine that buzzes, and a +12 st shift pushes the decoder into an F0 region where sine-excited HiFi-GAN measurably collapses) plus one unattributed live-mic "stutter" that the project has never tested on a raw microphone. As of October 2026 nothing open, Windows-viable and MIT/Apache-licensed beats RVC v2 at ~100 ms: Seed-VC is archived and 430 ms, X-VC (MIT, Apr 2026) is structurally 240 ms, MeanVC2 (Apache, Jun 2026) is 16 kHz and Mandarin-centric, and the sub-80 ms causal systems (SynthVC, TVTSyn, StreamVC) have no code. The right plan is three-tiered: first spend two weeks on measurement and inference-time fixes that need no retraining (gate/stutter attribution on a raw mic, decoupling `protect` from F0 interpolation, index retrieval as the leakage control, median-matched auto-pitch, a graph-safe FCPE to buy ~5 ms per hop and spend it on right context); second, retrain the target voice in Applio on breath-preserving, register-covering data with RefineGAN and/or SPIN-v2 while converting the decoder to carried streaming state; third, as the architectural bet, distil a tiny causal any-to-one student from the improved RVC teacher on the user's own microphone (the LLVC/SynthVC recipe), which the 2025–2026 evidence says is the only path to sub-50 ms without SOLA while keeping identity. Several of the most important claims below (RefineGAN at +12 st, index_rate vs leakage, FCPE vs RMVPE on this voice) have no controlled study anywhere and must be settled by the user's ear with the existing `vox bench` harness.

## The engine already runs at the GPU floor; the open problem is quality attribution, not speed

Every number in the repo says the inference stack has been squeezed about as far as the RVC architecture allows on this card. A 20 ms hop passes through a CPU input chain (70 Hz HPF, optional RNNoise, AGC to −20 dBFS, soft limiter), a Silero/energy gate, one pinned upload, six GPU stages each replayed as its own CUDA graph (resample, RMVPE on a 310 ms tail, ContentVec over the whole ~1.05 s window, GPU brute-force k-NN, synth, post) plus a graph-captured SOLA, and one device-to-host copy that is the hop's only sync ([src/vox/engine/pipeline.py](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/src/vox/engine/pipeline.py)). Under replay the stages cost **RMVPE 7.9 ms, ContentVec 3.0 ms, synth 4.8 ms, SOLA 0.2 ms**, host launch work is ~0.6 ms, and the 10-minute soak ran 30,047 hops with zero underruns ([docs/DEVIATIONS.md](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/docs/DEVIATIONS.md)). Compute is roughly 12% of the end-to-end latency; the rest is hop (20) + overlap (20) + search (10) + ring fill (40–50) + WASAPI device buffers (~22 ms per shared stream). This matches NVIDIA's own account of why Windows pipelines are launch-bound: average kernel-launch overhead under WDDM is "10+ microseconds" versus 3–5 µs on Linux, and graphs collapse that into one submission ([NVIDIA forums](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/any-way-to-measure-the-latency-of-a-kernel-launch/221413); [NVIDIA WSL2 blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/leveling-up-cuda-performance-on-wsl2-with-new-enhancements)). Hop 10 ms is physically impossible on this GPU (361 underruns in 20 s), and the one attempted whole-hop single graph hangs on replay whenever ContentVec and synth share a graph, which NVIDIA's troubleshooting taxonomy files under "process hang: no error, very hard" and attributes to un-joined side streams, external event dependencies or pool-order violations ([NVIDIA CUDA Graph troubleshooting](https://docs.nvidia.com/dl-cuda-graph/latest/troubleshooting/introduction.html)).

What must be preserved is therefore most of the engine: the sync-free hop discipline, per-stage graphs, the tensor-native graph-safe RMVPE (100% voicing agreement, 0.001 cent max deviation vs rvc-webui), the exact GPU k-NN (verified against FAISS), the `decoder_context_ms = 80` / `sine_phase_carry` / `noise_roll` / `f0_trim_tail = 2` "coherent hop" set, the 1000 ms ContentVec history (the R4 level-bias measurement showed 500 ms inflates pauses by +2.8 dB because ContentVec normalises over its window), the auto-pitch register tracker, the soft F0 ceiling, the input conditioner, and above all the benchmark harness: 15 `vox bench` subcommands, 30 wired A/B variants, jsonl baselines and a stream simulator that drives the real engine ([benchmarks/bench.py](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/benchmarks/bench.py)). The streaming recipe itself is also validated externally: a 2025 Interspeech study shows an unmodified HuBERT-Base run as a sliding window with ~2 s history and 400 ms lookahead loses only 1.8 pp phone error (11.9% to 13.7%) against full context, so the ≥1 s window DEXAIV re-encodes each hop is the right order of magnitude and should not be shortened ([Meng, Goldwater, Tang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22487v1)).

The project's own evidence base is strong on performance and thin on quality, and the audit is candid about it. There is no speaker-similarity, WER, MOS or blind-A/B number anywhere in the repo; the three coherent-hop defaults were chosen "by the user's ear, not on the report's reasoning" and are objectively "no measurable change"; the seam metric and the log-mel distance "both preferred the renders the user liked least" ([docs/WHAT_I_NEED_TO_DEBUG.md](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/docs/WHAT_I_NEED_TO_DEBUG.md)). The one dominant complaint, that every mic render of the speech script "stutters BADLY" while typed TTS renders never stutter, remains unattributed; the docs' candidate list is gate cuts, AGC gain ramps, 20 ms seam density and the two-render blend at overlap == hop, and the real take `take_20261015_122550` shows the gate opening 27 times in 86 s and closing 0.5–0.8 s into pauses ([takes/README.md](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/takes/README.md)). Two facts about that complaint matter for everything that follows. First, "raw_input" (no conditioning chain) "stutters much less but still rough", which points at the gate/AGC before the seams. Second, the engine "has never been run on a raw microphone by the user: every take so far is the NVIDIA Broadcast virtual mic", so an unknown neural denoiser sits in front of every quality judgement ever made. The published cost of such front ends is not small: neural suppressors lowered the P.835 signal-quality score (nSIG 3.87 → 3.77–3.79) and raised ASR WER on every test set including clean speech (5.7% → 5.9%), and "only one" third-DNS-challenge entry did not degrade SIG on average ([Braun & Tashev 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.11606)). A second subtlety the audit surfaces but the prose docs never discuss: the `tuned` records show the FP16 HuBERT ladder *failing* its eager-match check on both real voices at the default shape (`Mom|hop20`: fp16 L1 0.077, bf16 0.355 → `hubert_autocast: off`), so the precision actually in use is model-dependent and FP16 "buys nothing measurable" anyway (3.25 ms vs 2.6–3.0 ms) ([profiles/default/runtime.json](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/profiles/default/runtime.json)).

## Nine symptoms trace to four mechanisms, and only some need retraining

The user's list of artifacts collapses onto a small set of causes, each of which the code or the literature pins down. The table separates fixes that touch only inference from those that require retraining the target `.pth`.

Two of these deserve expansion because they are the largest levers and the least understood in the community. The first is the M→F register problem. The perception literature is consistent: F0 ranks first and formants a close second, speakers volitionally feminising raise F0 from 107.6 to 152.3 Hz but widen formant spacing only 5.5%, below the ≈6% ΔF threshold, and male voices are identified most reliably below ~200 Hz ([Cartei et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3281965); [Lavan et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6514200)). The decoder supplies the formants, so a feminine result requires (a) F0 clearly above 200 Hz and (b) a decoder that was *trained* in that region. The vocoder literature shows what happens otherwise: at a 2.0× F0 transform (exactly +12 st) HiFi-GAN+Sine, the NSF-style design RVC uses, falls to MOS 2.69 with 19% V/UV error, against 3.05 for SiFi-GAN and 3.23 for hn-uSFGAN ([SiFi-GAN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.15533v3)). DEXAIV's own data agree: the Sing preset had to drop transposition by 5 st and add a 360 Hz soft ceiling, which pulls peaks from 357 to 341 Hz. The evidence-aligned inference fix is median matching (shift so the *source's* phrase median lands on the *target's* median, as SVCC 2023 baseline B01 and T13 did), which DEXAIV's auto-pitch already implements but with a 200 Hz fallback because no model carries `target_f0_median_hz` (open question Q13). The retraining fix is target data recorded across the register the converted voice will actually occupy.

The second is source leakage. ContentVec reduced HuBERT's 81.4% SUPERB speaker-ID accuracy by up to 36% relative but its authors admit "some speaker information remains", and the 2026 USCF measurement puts its same-phoneme speaker EER at 27.98% (where higher is better) against 35.14% for kNN-normalised WavLM, while ContentVec has the *lowest* phoneme EER (8.82%) of the four encoders compared ([ContentVec](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09224); [USCF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08977)). That pairing explains the M→F failure mode exactly: ContentVec is the most phonetically faithful option, so consonants survive, but enough male timbre rides through that the decoder paints feminine formants over a masculine skeleton. RVC's `.index` retrieval is functionally the kNN-VC mechanism (replace each source frame with a weighted mean of the target's own training frames), which is why raising `index_rate` is the one leakage control available without retraining; kNN replacement raised speaker EER from 21.8% to 35.1% on WavLM at ~1 pp phoneme cost ([kNN-VC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18975v1); [USCF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.08977)). DEXAIV runs `index_rate = 0` by default and the user heard "no difference" between 0.35 and 0.6, but that test was on the `Mom` index at +12 with the Broadcast chain in front; a measured source-cosine sweep on a raw mic is still missing, and the sibilance cost at low `protect` is real (unvoiced frames are where source timbre is most audible).

## Nothing open beats RVC v2 at 100 ms yet, but two distinct successors are now worth prototyping

The architecture survey across the 2025–2026 literature yields an uncomfortable but useful result for a project that must stay conversational. RVC v2 remains the only mature, MIT-licensed, Windows-native stack that fine-tunes on ~10 minutes and runs at ~90–170 ms end to end; "RVC v3" is still only announced, and Applio, which supplied the meaningful upgrades (RefineGAN pretrains, 24 kHz RefineGAN, FCPE as realtime default, ASIO, phase-vocoder crossfades, TF32 training) has formally entered maintenance mode ([RVC-Project README](https://github.com/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI); [Applio releases](https://github.com/IAHispano/Applio/releases); [Applio README](https://github.com/IAHispano/Applio)). The ecosystem is on a plateau; further gains come from the training recipe and from this engine, not from upstream.

The through-line of the 2026 streaming papers is that specialising on a fixed target beats zero-shot conditioning every time it was measured, and that fully causal, state-carrying designs process only new samples and therefore need no SOLA: StreamVC, LLVC, RT-VC and SynthVC describe no stitching at all ([RT-VC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10289); [SynthVC](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09245v1)). SynthVC's 14.7M fixed-target converter trained on parallel pairs generated by Seed-VC beat zero-shot Seed-VC on speaker cosine at 77 ms on a laptop CPU core, and LLVC did the same with RVC as the teacher; together they form a recipe tailored to a one-user, one-target voice changer. The cost is that the student's quality is capped by its teacher and that no code exists for SynthVC/TVTSyn, so DEXAIV would be building, not integrating.

On content features, the survey's verdict is blunt. A raw Whisper-small or WavLM-Large swap is dominated: raw WavLM leaks *more* speaker identity than ContentVec (21.8% vs 28.0% EER), Whisper's encoder pads to a 30 s window, Seed-VC itself labels its Whisper model "offline" and uses XLSR-tiny for real time, and any non-ContentVec feature space means a new decoder anyway ([openai/whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper); [Seed-VC README](https://github.com/Plachtaa/seed-vc/blob/main/README.md)). The cheapest documented improvement on the ContentVec recipe is SPIN: it fine-tunes only HuBERT's last two layers with the same formant/F0 perturbation, costs 45 minutes on one A5000 (versus 19 h × 36 GPUs for ContentVec), lowers the last-layer speaker-ID probe to ~10% and improves PER, and `spin`/`spin-v2` are already first-class Applio embedders of identical HuBERT-Base shape, so DEXAIV's loader, CUDA graphs and 768-d index tooling would not change ([SPIN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11072); [Applio embedder docs](https://mintlify.wiki/IAHispano/Applio/advanced/embedder-models)). The caveat is explicit: no controlled A/B of SPIN vs ContentVec inside RVC exists, and Applio warns that any embedder mismatch between training and inference produces "buzzing, unnatural consonants, or reduced intelligibility", so SPIN is a retrain decision, never a hot swap.

On F0 trackers, FCPE is the practical 2026 default for streaming: 10.64M parameters versus RMVPE's 90.42M, RTF 0.0062 versus 0.0329 on a 4090 (5.3× cheaper), equal or better clean accuracy on Mandarin speech (RPA 97.56 vs 96.37), but markedly worse under heavy noise (−20 dB white: 29.75 vs 43.63) ([FCPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15140v1)). DEXAIV measured FCPE at 3.1 ms versus RMVPE's 13.8 ms eager, but the stock `torchfcpe` is not graph-safe and was never A/B'd on this voice (Q2). The lightweight newcomers, SwiftF0 at 95,842 parameters with the best 10 dB-SNR octave accuracy, PESTO at ~130k parameters with <10 ms latency, and FCNF0++ at a 10 ms hop and 408× realtime, are credible but none reports accuracy on 70–120 Hz male voices and none ships in any RVC toolchain ([SwiftF0](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18440v1); [PESTO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01488); [PENN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.12258)).

On vocoders, the decisive fact is that nothing is a drop-in: RVC's `net_g.dec` consumes the 192-channel flow latent, not a mel, so BigVGAN-v2, Vocos, HiFT and PeriodWave cannot replace it without retraining, and Applio's RefineGAN "requires the Applio client and matching pretrained weights" because the decoder is trained jointly ([ApplioX synthesizers.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/IAHispano/ApplioX/blob/main/rvc/lib/algorithm/synthesizers.py); [Applio training docs](https://mintlify.wiki/IAHispano/Applio/features/training)). Among retrain options RefineGAN has the strongest published lift (MOS +0.7 on English speech, +1.6 on Chinese singing) and is FP16-safe, HiFTNet shows the harmonic+noise source is worth −1.116 CMOS, Vocos-class iSTFT heads have the best periodicity and an inherent overlap-add that makes chunk boundaries cheap but need LayerNorm → BatchNorm for streaming, and MS-Wavehax reaches top MOS with one frame of lookahead at 0.33M parameters ([Comparative analysis of streaming vocoders](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03554v1)). The one vocoder-adjacent item that needs no retraining is AP-BWE: Vonovox, the closed-source Windows RVC app running on exactly torch 2.7 / CUDA 12.8 / Python 3.12, ships a realtime-modified AP-BWE upscaler to 48 kHz plus compiled BigVGAN and SiFiGAN modules, which is the only evidence those kernels build on this toolchain ([dr87/Vonovox](https://github.com/dr87/Vonovox)).

On the inference stack, the ranking is equally clear. Keep hand-rolled per-stage `torch.cuda.CUDAGraph` as the backbone (the only path with first-party support on Windows), chain them through static device buffers per NVIDIA's checklist ("chain graph outputs as inputs to next graph", "replay order matches capture order"), and pre-allocate every per-hop buffer because allocation overhead on WDDM is "an order of magnitude higher" than launch ([NVIDIA quick checklist](https://docs.nvidia.com/dl-cuda-graph/latest/torch-cuda-graph/quick-checklist.html)). TensorRT-RTX 1.7 is the one runtime worth a time-boxed trial: under 200 MB, JIT in under 30 s, CC 12.0 in its default portability set, built-in CUDA graphs, WDDM-only (fine for GeForce), but FP8 deconvolutions are unsupported (HiFi-GAN's upsamplers), Multinomial/NonZero are unsupported (noise must be an input), the torch backend is "experimental", the ORT plugin's PyPI wheels are "coming soon", and no cp313 win_amd64 wheel was verifiable ([TensorRT-RTX docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt-rtx/latest/index.html); [1.6 release notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt-rtx/latest/getting-started/release-notes-1/1.6.html); [ORT TensorRT RTX EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/TensorRTRTX-ExecutionProvider)). The cautionary datapoint is TRT-SE, where a frame-by-frame TensorRT engine ran at 2.2 ms per frame versus 1.0 ms on CPU ONNX Runtime because transfer overhead dominated ([TRT-SE](https://github.com/Xiaobin-Rong/TRT-SE)); at DEXAIV's 4.8 ms synth stage the fusion win must exceed the handoff cost or it is a loss. torch.compile on Windows CUDA exists only via community `triton-windows` (torch 2.7 ↔ Triton 3.3, Blackwell supported, Python 3.13 not listed, `reduce-overhead` undocumented) ([triton-windows](https://github.com/woct0rdho/triton-windows)). On audio I/O, PortAudio's WASAPI backend does not use `IAudioClient3`; its maintainer measured a ~22 ms shared-mode floor at 48 kHz, while native low-latency shared mode reached under 20 ms round trip and PortAudio exclusive 13 ms with crackling ([PortAudio #385](https://github.com/PortAudio/portaudio/issues/385)). DEXAIV already measured exclusive output as the 72 ms configuration and found the Realtek mic broken in exclusive mode, so a native `IAudioClient3` input path is the remaining I/O lever.

## A three-phase plan that keeps the engine conversational at every step

The plan below is ordered so that each phase is useful on its own and produces the measurements the next phase needs. Tags: gain and risk are qualitative judgements from the evidence above; latency impact is algorithmic unless noted; "retrain" means the target `.pth` must be retrained (which presupposes access to the target's dataset, itself unresolved as Q15).

### Do not do

Do not swap raw Whisper or WavLM-Large features in front of the existing `.pth` (more compute, no leakage benefit, incompatible feature space). Do not adopt Seed-VC V1 for live use (archived, GPL-3.0, 430 ms) or Vevo2 (CC-BY-NC-ND), GPT-SoVITS VC or Chatterbox VC (offline only). Do not pursue ONNX Runtime's CUDA EP for its own sake: it imposes the same CUDA-graph constraints plus a framework boundary ([ORT CUDA EP](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/CUDA-ExecutionProvider.html)). Do not retry the whole-hop single graph until a side-stream/event/pool-order diagnosis is in hand, and do not rely on `torch.compile(mode="reduce-overhead")` on Windows. Do not attempt hop 10 ms on this GPU. Do not try to load BigVGAN-v2, Vocos or RefineGAN weights into an existing RVC decoder; no converter exists. Do not use FP8 on the decoder (no FP8 deconvolutions in TensorRT-RTX) or BF16 anywhere the project already measured it losing (L1 0.355 on Mom). Do not lower SineGen's unvoiced-noise constants or make voicing soft on existing weights (train/inference mismatch). Do not add de-essers or spectral smoothers as a quality fix; no evidence supports them and they trade brightness for masking. Do not run RNNoise behind NVIDIA Broadcast, and do not use Applio-style formant shift beyond a few percent (it stretches sibilant centroids and the noise floor). Do not run a GPU TTS engine beside the live engine without the hold-before-render change: every bake-off GPU engine starved the live engine (35–890 underruns per 8 lines) ([docs/TTS_NOTES.md](https://github.com/RheaIsCute/DEXAIV/blob/main/docs/TTS_NOTES.md)).

### Open questions that need the user's ear or a prototype

Is the stutter the gate, the AGC ramp, or the seams, and does it survive a raw microphone? Does `index_rate` 0.3–0.5 reduce "man with a high voice" more than it blurs sibilants on this voice? Is FCPE's voicing good enough on this talker's 67 Hz floor behind Broadcast, and on a raw mic? Is 20–40 ms of right context audible as a consonant improvement, and is the extra latency acceptable in conversation? Does RefineGAN's MOS lift survive a +12 st transposition, a question no paper has asked? Does the training dataset for `Mom`/`GoatMommy` exist at all (Q15)? Does the user hear the 48 kHz AP-BWE band on the 32k models as air or as hiss? Do X-VC and MeanVC2 hold their published similarity on English male speech into a feminine target, and does the user accept 240 ms for a markedly more feminine voice in a push-to-talk mode? Finally, is decoder FP16 audible at all (Q6)? Each of these is a `vox bench` variant plus a blind pair; none is settled by the literature.

## Conclusion

The research reframes DEXAIV's problem. The engine has reached the point where the artifacts it produces are the RVC recipe's artifacts, reproduced faithfully and fast, and the handful of streaming-specific ones left (overlap == hop blend, gate chatter) are either already fixed by ear or attributable with one raw-mic session. That means the leverage order inverts from what a performance-minded project expects: data and target-model retraining (register coverage, breath, RefineGAN/SPIN) now dominate any runtime change, and the inference-time wins that remain are about *where* features and pitch land (median matching, index retrieval, protect/interp decoupling, right context) rather than how fast they are computed. The two numbers to internalise are 27.98% (how much of the source speaker ContentVec still carries) and 2.69 (HiFi-GAN+Sine's MOS at a 2× F0 transform): together they are why a +12 st RVC voice is "a man with a high voice", and neither is addressed by any 2025–2026 fork of RVC.

The strategic implication is that DEXAIV's single-user, single-target constraint, which looks like a limitation, is actually its advantage. Every 2026 streaming result that beats zero-shot systems on identity did so by specialising on a fixed target, and the sub-80 ms systems did so by going fully causal with carried state. Nobody has released that combination as software, but the recipe (a good teacher, the user's own mic data, a small causal student) is documented and within reach of a project that already owns a bit-exact RVC implementation, a CUDA-graph pipeline and a benchmark harness. Harden the teacher first; the successor is distilled from it.
